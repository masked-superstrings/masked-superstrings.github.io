About

What are masked superstrings?
k-mer set (k = 3)GGAACCCGAGACACTCTT↓superstringGGACCGACTTmask1110101100occurrencesGGA GAC ACC CCGnot in the set CGA GACduplicate ACT CTT

A masked superstring is a string containing every k-mer of a set, plus a binary mask.

Each 1 in the mask switches on the k-mer starting at that position; the other occurrences are ignored.

Here, GGACCGACTT + 1110101100 represents {GGA, ACC, CGA, GAC, ACT, CTT}.

Masked superstrings generalize SPSS

Superstrings correspond to paths in the k-mer overlap graph, of which the de Bruijn graph is the subgraph of (k – 1)-overlaps. Concatenating the strings of any SPSS therefore gives a valid masked superstring, and every SPSS construction is a constrained superstring heuristic. Overlaps shorter than k – 1 allow even shorter superstrings:

Masks are not unique: they can be chosen to avoid duplicate k-mers or to make the mask more compressible.

Masked superstrings can be computed efficiently

BiGreedy adapts the classical greedy superstring algorithm to genomic k-mer sets: it repeatedly merges the two sequences with the longest suffix–prefix overlap, accounting for reverse complements. Because any duplicate-free SPSS is a valid intermediate state, BiGreedy can start from unitigs or simplitigs computed by parallel tools such as GGCAT, and the resulting superstrings keep them as retrievable substrings.

As a result, computing masked superstrings is similar to computing unitigs: most of the time goes to unitig construction, and the additional BiGreedy step is cheap. With the parallel implementation, the whole computation takes 1.0–1.3× the time of unitig construction alone.

BiGreedy is implemented in KmerCamel and, in parallel, in Pandedup.

BiGreedy superstrings are near-optimal

How short can a superstring be? A minimum-weight cycle cover of the k-mer overlap graph gives a lower bound on the length of any superstring of a k-mer set. Across k-mer sizes, pangenome expansion, abundance filtering and subsampling, BiGreedy superstrings exceed this bound by a median of 0.004% and by less than 0.05% in all tested conditions.

Unitigs, simplitigs and matchtigs get close to the bound only where the de Bruijn graph is contiguous. Where it branches or fragments, they approach the trivial representation with one string per k-mer, whereas BiGreedy superstrings remain near-optimal.

Masked superstrings improve compression

Compressed masked superstrings (superstring plus mask) are typically smaller than compressed SPSS and ESS-Compress across genomes, pangenomes and raw-read k-mer sets, with the largest gains for subsampled sets. Jointly optimizing superstring length and mask further improves the compression of pangenome k-mer sets (Pareto optimization).

The BMS storage format combines a BiGreedy superstring, its mask, unitig boundaries and unitig abundances. On a randomized benchmark of accessions from the Logan archive, it reduced the compressed size of unitigs with abundances several-fold, while recovering them exactly.

Masked superstrings improve indexing

The masked Burrows-Wheeler transform (MBWT) of a masked superstring yields FMSI, a memory-efficient exact k-mer index supporting membership queries, compressed dictionary queries, and set operations through function-assigned masked superstrings. Because BiGreedy superstrings retain unitigs and simplitigs as substrings, they also integrate with existing SPSS-based indexes such as SSHash.

FMSI2 keeps the memory footprint of FMSI (3.6–7.1 bits per k-mer in its two smaller presets) and answers queries more than an order of magnitude faster, which places it on the Pareto front of memory and query time among the compared indexes.

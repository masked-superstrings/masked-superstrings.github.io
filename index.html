About

What are masked superstrings?
k-mer set (k = 3)GGAACCCGAGACACTCTT↓superstringGGACCGACTTmask1110101100occurrencesGGA GAC ACC CCGnot in the set CGA GACduplicate ACT CTT

A masked superstring is a string containing every k-mer of a set, plus a binary mask.

Each 1 in the mask switches on the k-mer starting at that position; the other occurrences are ignored.

Here, GGACCGACTT + 1110101100 represents {GGA, ACC, CGA, GAC, ACT, CTT}, written in mask-cased form as GGAcCgACtt.

Masked superstrings generalize SPSS

Superstrings correspond to paths in the k-mer overlap graph, of which the de Bruijn graph is the subgraph of (k – 1)-overlaps. Concatenating the strings of any SPSS therefore gives a valid masked superstring, and every SPSS construction is a constrained superstring heuristic. Overlaps shorter than k – 1 allow even shorter superstrings:

Masks are not unique: they can be chosen to avoid duplicate k-mers or to make the mask more compressible.

Masked superstrings can be easily and efficiently computed

BiGreedy adapts the classical greedy superstring algorithm to genomic k-mer sets: it repeatedly merges the two sequences with the longest suffix–prefix overlap, accounting for reverse complements. Because any duplicate-free SPSS is a valid intermediate state, BiGreedy can start from unitigs or simplitigs computed by parallel tools such as GGCAT, adding little time over unitig construction, and the resulting superstrings keep them as retrievable substrings.

Across k-mer sizes, pangenome expansion, abundance filtering and subsampling, BiGreedy superstrings stay very close to a lower bound on superstring length, and are substantially shorter than simplitigs and matchtigs where the de Bruijn graph branches or fragments. BiGreedy is implemented in KmerCamel and, in parallel, in Pandedup.

Masked superstrings improve compression

Compressed masked superstrings (superstring plus mask) are smaller than compressed SPSS and ESS-Compress across genomes, pangenomes and raw-read k-mer sets, with the largest gains for subsampled sets. Jointly optimizing superstring length and mask further improves the compression of pangenome k-mer sets (Pareto optimization).

The BMS storage format combines a BiGreedy superstring, its mask, unitig boundaries and unitig abundances. On a randomized benchmark of accessions from the Logan archive, it reduced the compressed size of unitigs with abundances several-fold, while recovering them exactly.

Masked superstrings improve indexing

The masked Burrows-Wheeler transform (MBWT) of a masked superstring yields FMSI, a memory-efficient exact k-mer index supporting membership queries, compressed dictionary queries, and set operations through function-assigned masked superstrings. Because BiGreedy superstrings retain unitigs and simplitigs as substrings, they also integrate with existing SPSS-based indexes such as SSHash.
